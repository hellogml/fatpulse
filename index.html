Why it works

The Navy method reads your waist, not your wallet

Research-grade body fat tests (DEXA, hydrostatic) cost time and money. The U.S. Navy circumference method uses age, sex and tape-measure figures to match clinical estimates within a few points — accurate enough to track monthly progress.

  1. Measure neck, waist and (for women) hip at their widest point.
  2. Convert inches to the formula — FatPulse does this automatically.
  3. Compare against ACE ranges so you know whether you're in "fitness" or "acceptable".

Body fat basics

Is body fat percentage better than BMI?

Generally yes for tracking body composition. BMI only compares weight to height and can't tell muscle from fat, so a lean athlete score can look "overweight". Body fat percentage measures what's actually fat versus lean tissue.

How accurate is the U.S. Navy method?

Within about 3 percentage points of DEXA for most people when measurements are taken correctly. Consistency matters more than precision — measure the same spot at the same time of day to see true trends.

How fast can I safely lower body fat?

A target of roughly 0.5–1% of body weight per week (a 250–500 kcal daily deficit) preserves muscle and is widely considered sustainable. Plan on losing about 0.5–1% body fat per month as a realistic benchmark.

What's a healthy body fat for a man vs. a woman?

Essential levels are about 2–5% for men and 10–13% for women. Athletic ranges run 6–13% (men) and 14–20% (women); acceptable zones are roughly 18–24% (men) and 25–31% (women).
